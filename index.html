Quick recap: the foundations Usul al-Din and Furu' al-Din, the ground everything below stands on

Usul al-Din · Roots of Religion

  1. Tawhid: the Oneness of Allah
  2. 'Adl: Allah's Justice
  3. Nubuwwa: Prophethood, sealed by Muhammad ﷺ
  4. Imama: the twelve Imams chosen by Allah to lead after the Prophet
  5. Ma'ad: the Day of Resurrection

Furu' al-Din · Branches of Religion

  1. Salat: prayer
  2. Sawm: fasting
  3. Hajj: pilgrimage
  4. Zakat: alms
  5. Khums: one-fifth of yearly surplus
  6. Jihad: striving in Allah's way
  7. Amr bil-Ma'ruf: enjoining good
  8. Nahy 'anil-Munkar: forbidding evil
  9. Tawalli: loving Allah's friends
  10. Tabarri: dissociating from Allah's enemies

Why the history matters: the fourth root, Imama, is a belief about history. Every story below is an answer to one question: after the Prophet, who was meant to guide the Ummah, and what happened to them?

    Where to go next

    Primary texts

    • Nahj al-Balagha: sermons, letters and sayings of Imam Ali
    • al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya: the supplications of Imam Zayn al-Abidin
    • Kitab al-Irshad, Shaykh al-Mufid: the lives of the twelve Imams
    • al-Luhuf, Sayyid Ibn Tawus: the classic account of Karbala

    Modern books

    • Shi'ite Islam, Allamah Tabatabai: a clear introduction
    • The Succession to Muhammad, Wilferd Madelung: an academic study of the early caliphate
    • Then I Was Guided, Muhammad al-Tijani: one man's journey to Shia Islam
    • Peshawar Nights, Sultan al-Wa'izin Shirazi: a famous series of debates

    Online

    • al-islam.org: free Shia library, including most books above
    • thaqalayn.net: searchable Shia hadith collections in English
    • WikiShia: encyclopedia of Shia topics
    • sistani.org: practical rulings (as one example of a marja')

    A note on how this history is told

    These stories follow Shia sources, and they name the Sunni sources that agree, including Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, wherever they exist. Where Sunni Muslims read events differently, the story says so plainly.

    Knowing this history is not a licence to insult anyone. Imam Ali advised the caliphs, named his sons Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman, and told his soldiers at Siffin not to curse the Syrians but to describe their deeds. Senior Shia scholars today forbid insulting what Sunni Muslims hold sacred, and Ayatollah Sistani is known for saying of Sunni Muslims: do not call them our brothers, they are ourselves.

    Learn with a teacher. A local 'alim or a centre linked to your marja' can guide you through the details of belief and practice.
